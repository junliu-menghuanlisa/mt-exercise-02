AI-ccent Judgements:
Accent Stereotypes Persist with AI-Generated Voices

Jun Liu | University of Zurich | IAFPA 2026

Department of Computational Linguistics | jun.liu@uzh.ch

Listeners evaluate AI-generated voices not only through acoustic realism, but also through sociolinguistic meanings attached to accent.

Background

AI-generated voices are increasingly realistic but are not necessarily perceived as socially neutral. Listeners use accent to infer identity, credibility, competence, and social character. Accent stereotypes may also shape judgements of speaker traits and behaviour in forensic contexts (Paver et al., 2025), while increasingly realistic synthetic speech creates challenges involving deception, fraud, impersonation, and audio-deepfake detection (Almutairi & Elgibreen, 2022).

Research Gap

Little is known about how accent, voice gender, and listener background jointly shape social evaluations of voices that listeners know are AI-generated.

Research Questions

  1. RQ1 — Accent Type
    Do standard and regional AI-generated accents differ in perceived trustworthiness, professionalism, and human-likeness?
  2. RQ2 — Voice Gender
    Does perceived voice gender influence these evaluations?
  3. RQ3 — Listener and Accent Nationality
    Do British and American listeners evaluate British- and American-accented AI voices differently?

Method

Stimuli

16 ElevenLabs-generated voices
64 audio clips
4 accent varieties

Accent type: Standard / Regional
Accent nationality: British / American
Perceived voice gender: Female / Male

Participants

40 native English-speaking listeners

20 British
20 American

Rating Task

All participants rated all 64 clips in individually randomised order.

Four 6-point scales:

  • Trustworthiness
  • Professionalism
  • Human-likeness
  • Perceived audio naturalness

Dataset

40 participants × 64 clips
= 2,560 observations

Statistical Analysis

Beta mixed-effects regression

  • Ratings rescaled to the open interval (0,1)
  • Fixed effects: accent type, voice gender, listener nationality, accent nationality, and listener nationality × accent nationality
  • Crossed random intercepts: participant and audio file
  • Estimates reported on the logit scale

Supplementary analyses

  • Self-reported AI exposure: robustness check
  • Perceived audio naturalness: supplementary perceptual predictor

Key Findings

1 Accent Type Effect

Regional accents were rated significantly higher than standard accents for trustworthiness and human-likeness, but not professionalism.

Outcome Estimate p
Trustworthiness b = 0.217 .009
Professionalism b = −0.058 .460
Human-likeness b = 0.474 < .001

Regional accents showed advantages in trustworthiness and human-likeness.

2 Accent Nationality Effect

British-accented voices were rated as more trustworthy and professional by both British and American listeners.

Trustworthiness

British listeners: b = 0.395, p < .001
American listeners: b = 0.310, p < .001

Professionalism

British listeners: b = 0.232, p = .009
American listeners: b = 0.331, p < .001

Human-likeness

British listeners: b = 0.639, p < .001
American listeners: b = 0.182, p = .160

The listener nationality × accent nationality interaction was significant only for human-likeness: b = −0.114, p < .001.

The results did not support a symmetrical own-accent preference.

3 Voice Gender Effect

Voice gender did not significantly predict any of the three outcomes in the main models.

Trustworthiness: b = −0.019, p = .815

Professionalism: b = −0.106, p = .177

Human-likeness: b = 0.060, p = .622

Voice gender was a comparatively weak evaluative cue in this disembodied AI-voice context.

Supplementary Analyses

A. AI Exposure

Self-reported AI exposure was included as a robustness-check predictor.

Self-reported AI exposure was included as a robustness check. Its inclusion did not materially alter the main pattern of findings.

B. Perceived Audio Naturalness

Trustworthiness: b = 1.037, p < .001
Professionalism: b = 0.776, p < .001
Human-likeness: b = 1.578, p < .001

Perceived audio naturalness positively predicted all three outcomes, most strongly human-likeness.

Perceived audio naturalness was measured rather than experimentally manipulated; these relationships are associative, not causal.

Overall Pattern

Accent was a stronger evaluative cue than voice gender, while perceived audio naturalness was strongly associated with all three social evaluations.

Discussion

Synthetic voices were not evaluated as socially neutral. Accent continued to carry social meaning even when listeners knew that the voices were artificially generated.

The effects were dimension-specific: no single voice profile simultaneously optimised trustworthiness, professionalism, and human-likeness.

Human-likeness was particularly sensitive to accent type, listener background, and perceived audio naturalness.

Implications

  • Standard accents should not automatically be treated as neutral or superior defaults.
  • AI voice selection should be tested with intended listener groups.
  • Voice localisation cannot rely only on matching accent nationality to listener nationality.
  • AI voice technologies may reproduce existing sociolinguistic hierarchies.
  • Evaluation frameworks should distinguish trustworthiness, professionalism, human-likeness, and perceived audio naturalness.

Forensic Relevance

Accent stereotypes can affect perceptions of speaker traits and behaviour in forensic contexts (Paver et al., 2025). Increasingly realistic synthetic voices also create challenges for detecting impersonation and audio deepfakes (Almutairi & Elgibreen, 2022).

The present findings suggest that judgements of synthetic-voice authenticity and credibility may reflect sociolinguistic expectations as well as perceived audio quality.

This study did not directly test forensic evidence evaluation or legal decision-making.

Limitations

  • Modest listener sample: 20 British and 20 American participants
  • Voices generated using one TTS platform
  • Possible confounding with voice-specific acoustic properties
  • Single-item 6-point measures
  • Isolated clips rather than interactive or forensic contexts

Conclusion

AI-generated voices are evaluated through both acoustic and social perception. Accent remains a powerful cue in how listeners judge synthetic speech.

Accent stereotypes persist—even when the voice is artificial.
