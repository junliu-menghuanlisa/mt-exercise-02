Accent Stereotypes Persist with AI-Generated Voices

Background

AI-generated voices are increasingly realistic, but they are not necessarily perceived as socially neutral. In human speech, listeners use accent to infer identity, credibility, competence, and social character. Accent stereotypes may also shape listeners’ judgements of social traits and behaviour in forensic contexts (Paver et al., 2025). Meanwhile, the growing realism of synthetic speech creates challenges involving deception, fraud, impersonation, and audio-deepfake detection (Almutairi & Elgibreen, 2022).

Research gap

Little is known about how accent, voice gender, and listener background jointly shape social evaluations of voices that listeners know are AI-generated.

Research Questions

  1. RQ1 — Accent Type
    Do standard and regional AI-generated accents differ in perceived trustworthiness, professionalism, and human-likeness?
  2. RQ2 — Voice Gender
    Does perceived voice gender influence these evaluations?
  3. RQ3 — Listener and Accent Nationality
    Do British and American listeners evaluate British- and American-accented AI voices differently?

Statistical Analysis: Beta Mixed-Effects Regression

Ratings were rescaled from the original 1–6 scale to the open interval (0,1) and analysed using beta mixed-effects regression. Estimates are reported on the logit scale.

Main-model fixed effects

  • Accent type
  • Voice gender
  • Listener nationality
  • Accent nationality
  • Listener nationality × Accent nationality

Crossed random intercepts

  • Participant
  • Audio file

Supplementary models

  • Self-reported AI exposure was added as a robustness check.
  • Standardised perceived audio naturalness was added in a separate supplementary analysis.
  • The main-model predictors and random-effects structure were retained.

Beta mixed-effects regression accommodated bounded ratings while accounting for repeated observations across listeners and audio stimuli.

Key Finding 1 — Accent Type

Regional accents sounded more trustworthy and more human-like.

Outcome Regional − Standard p Interpretation
Trustworthiness b = 0.217 .009 Regional advantage
Professionalism b = −0.058 .460 No reliable difference
Human-likeness b = 0.474 < .001 Regional advantage

Accent effects were dimension-specific: regionality increased trustworthiness and human-likeness, but did not significantly predict professionalism.

Key Finding 2 — Voice Gender

No reliable voice-gender effect

Outcome Female − Male p
Trustworthiness b = −0.019 .815
Professionalism b = −0.106 .177
Human-likeness b = 0.060 .622

Voice gender did not significantly predict any of the three outcomes in the main models, suggesting that it was a comparatively weak cue in this disembodied AI-voice context.

Key Finding 3 — Listener and Accent Nationality

British-accented voices showed a broader evaluative advantage.

Contrasts: British-accented − American-accented voices

Outcome British listeners American listeners
Trustworthiness b = 0.395, p < .001 b = 0.310, p < .001
Professionalism b = 0.232, p = .009 b = 0.331, p < .001
Human-likeness b = 0.639, p < .001 b = 0.182, p = .160

Interaction results

  • Trustworthiness interaction: p = .314
  • Professionalism interaction: p = .243
  • Human-likeness interaction: b = −0.114, p < .001

Both British and American listeners rated British-accented voices as more trustworthy and professional than American-accented voices. The findings therefore did not support a symmetrical own-accent preference.

For human-likeness, British listeners preferred British-accented voices, whereas American listeners showed no corresponding American-accent advantage.

B. Perceived Audio Naturalness

Perceived audio naturalness positively predicted all three outcomes.

Outcome Estimate p
Trustworthiness b = 1.037 < .001
Professionalism b = 0.776 < .001
Human-likeness b = 1.578 < .001

The strongest association was observed for human-likeness. Because perceived audio naturalness was measured rather than experimentally manipulated, these results should be interpreted as associations rather than causal effects.

AI-voice evaluation reflects both sociolinguistic meaning and perceived audio quality.

Discussion and Implications

  • Synthetic voices carry sociolinguistic meaning: listeners responded to accent-based social cues even when they knew the voices were AI-generated.
  • Evaluation is multidimensional: the same voice profile did not optimise trustworthiness, professionalism, and human-likeness simultaneously.
  • Standard does not necessarily mean superior: regional voices were perceived as more trustworthy and human-like.
  • Listener background matters, particularly for perceived human-likeness.
  • AI voice selection and localisation should be empirically tested with intended listener groups.

Forensic Relevance

Accent stereotypes can influence listeners’ perceptions of speaker traits and behaviour in forensic contexts (Paver et al., 2025). At the same time, increasingly realistic synthetic voices present challenges for detecting impersonation and audio deepfakes (Almutairi & Elgibreen, 2022). The present findings suggest that judgements of synthetic-voice authenticity and credibility may reflect sociolinguistic expectations as well as acoustic quality.

The present experiment examined social evaluations of AI-generated voices; it did not directly test forensic evidence evaluation or legal decision-making.

Limitations

  • Modest sample: 20 British and 20 American listeners
  • All voices came from one TTS platform
  • Accent and voice gender may have been partly confounded with voice-specific acoustic properties
  • Isolated clips were evaluated without an interactive or forensic context
  • Each evaluative construct was measured with a single 6-point item

Conclusion

AI-generated voices are not socially neutral. Regional accents were perceived as more trustworthy and human-like, while British-accented voices received broader advantages in trustworthiness and professionalism. AI-voice evaluation is shaped by sociolinguistic meaning, listener background, and perceived audio naturalness.
